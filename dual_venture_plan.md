# Dual Venture Plan
## How Patterns in Place and the B2B Tracking-Plan Consulting Practice Run Together

This doc is the operating recommendation for running both ventures concurrently with **Patterns in Place as primary** and the **B2B tracking-plan consulting practice as secondary**. It supersedes the original `multi_venture_strategy.md` in the consulting folder, which was written when Patterns in Place was scoped as a side project. Patterns in Place has since grown into a full publication operation, and the time math, channel architecture, and content cadence in the older doc no longer reflect reality.

Read this doc first. The venture-specific docs in each folder are still valid for their respective tactics — pillars, formats, methodology, consulting offers — but the integration logic, channel allocation, and time split live here.

---

## The Decision

After working through both ventures end to end:

- **Patterns in Place is primary.** It's the publication you want to spend your time writing for, the audience you want to build, and the body of work that creates the strongest interview narrative for the senior in-house roles you'd actually take.
- **The consulting practice runs secondary.** It exists as a small, premium-priced, low-volume B2B services business — 2–4 Foundation Builds per year at the upper end of pricing, plus the template pack as passive baseline. It does *not* have a content marketing engine.
- **The unifying brand is your name.** Both ventures live under it. Personal site is the umbrella. LinkedIn profile mentions both but leads with the publication.
- **Substack is the publication's home, not Medium.** This consolidates the long-form home and the newsletter into one surface and matches a publication-first identity better than Medium would.
- **LinkedIn is a showcase, not a feed.** Tool launches, big Metro Deep Dives, annual retrospectives, bridge pieces — not weekly Data Takes. Personal LinkedIn posting frequency drops to 1–2/month.
- **X and Bluesky carry the social distribution.** X primarily; Bluesky as the sister channel that catches the civic-tech and data viz audiences who've migrated off X.

The shape is: a publication with depth and discipline on Substack, a daily voice on X/Bluesky, a credibility surface on GitHub, interactive tools on Streamlit, and a low-frequency, high-signal LinkedIn showcase that doubles as the interview funnel. The consulting business runs in the background on warm-network outreach, GitHub-repo discoverability, and selective community presence — not feed marketing.

## Time Allocation

At a sustainable 15–20 hours per week, the split is roughly 70/30 in favor of Patterns in Place.

| Venture | Hours/week | What gets done |
|---|---|---|
| Patterns in Place | 10–14 | Pipeline work, tool deployments, Substack pieces, X/Bluesky posts, occasional LinkedIn showcase, newsletter |
| Consulting | 4–6 | Standards docs additions, monthly long-form, prospect calls, client delivery when active, template-pack maintenance |
| Buffer | 1–2 | Spillover, learning, admin |

When a paid Foundation Build is in flight, consulting absorbs 12–15 hours/week for the four weeks of delivery, and Patterns in Place runs at minimum cadence (one Data Take per week, nothing else). When the consulting calendar is empty, those hours flow into a tool deployment, an Opportunity List, or pipeline work.

The non-negotiable: a Data Take ships on Substack every week. Even in a heavy consulting week, this single piece of weekly cadence is what keeps the publication alive and compounding.

## Personal Brand Architecture

One umbrella, two venture surfaces. Your name is the brand; the publication is the front; the consulting practice is the back.

**Personal site (`yourname.com`).** The umbrella. Sections: hero with a one-line dual-venture statement, `/publication` linking to Patterns in Place, `/consulting` linking to the tracking-plan practice, `/writing` with a feed of Substack pieces and consulting long-forms, `/about` with the human story, and contact. Built on Carrd, Framer, or Astro — keep it light. Iterate constantly; don't agonize over v1.

**Personal LinkedIn profile.** Headline leads with Patterns in Place; About paragraph mentions the consulting practice in one sentence. Featured section pins the publication, the NYC tool, the Florida tool, one strong Metro Deep Dive, and the tracking-plan GitHub repo. Posting cadence: 1–2 posts/month — high-production, showcase-style. The profile is doing more work than the feed in this setup.

**LinkedIn pages.** One for Patterns in Place (create early; minimal upkeep). One for the consulting practice (defer until the consulting business has revenue justifying it).

**Two GitHub orgs.** `PatternsInPlace` for the publication's repos (pipeline, tools, scoring models, methodology, visual library, publication-index). The tracking-plan consulting repo lives separately under your personal account or a small consulting-named org — keep it physically separate from PatternsInPlace so the credibility surfaces don't cross-pollute.

**Substack publication (`patternsinplace.substack.com`).** The publication's home. Long-form, Data Takes, newsletter — all on one surface. Set the masthead, the About page, and the section structure (Place Stories, Comparisons, Opportunity Finder, Decision Guide, Data Takes — mirroring the five pillars).

**X (`@patternsinplace`).** Publication-branded social account. Daily voice. Posts under the publication's handle, not your personal one. Your byline links back to your name on the Substack pieces — pseudonymity at the handle level, attribution at the publication level.

**Bluesky (`@patternsinplace`).** Sister channel to X. Mirror via cross-poster initially; tune Bluesky-native if the audience there meaningfully diverges.

**Consulting site (`yourdomain.com` for the practice, or a sub-page under your personal site).** Single page with positioning, three packaged services with pricing, methodology link to GitHub, contact + Cal.com. Holds the long-form blog as a sub-section.

## Patterns in Place — Content Production

The five pillars and three formats from `editorial_strategy.md` carry over unchanged. The cadence trims modestly from the doc's full-stage targets to make room for the consulting work.

**Metro Deep Dives — every 6 weeks (~2,500 words).** The flagship format. Seven-section structure (thesis, population, economic structure, housing, affordability, sub-metro opportunity, takeaway). Ships as a coordinated package: Quarto notebook on GitHub, narrative version on Substack, X thread with the lead chart, second X thread three days later with a different finding, one LinkedIn post if the piece is strong enough to warrant it. First six in priority order: Jacksonville (already scoped), a peer-comparison piece, a Sun Belt vs. legacy-Midwest story, a metro that surprises, a regional-thesis piece, a follow-up.

**Opportunity Lists — monthly (~1,000 words).** Most shareable format. Five to twelve markets, one anchor number per item. Pulls from the post bank in `distribution_strategy.md` — wages-rising-faster-than-rents, healthy young-professional pipelines, where builders are quietly adding supply, where a $200k income goes furthest. These pieces drive the most newsletter signups.

**Data Takes — weekly (~700 words).** The cadence anchor and the non-negotiable. One question, one chart, one finding. Pull from the chatbot Q&A training set — that asset exists specifically to fuel this format. Even in a heavy consulting week, one Data Take ships.

**Interactive tools — every 6–8 weeks.** Order: NYC Neighborhood Explorer (week 2), Florida parcel tool (week 6), Overheating Index dashboard (month 3), Investment Score dashboard (month 4), chatbot public beta (month 5). Each tool is a coordinated launch — Substack piece + X thread + GitHub repo public + LinkedIn showcase post.

**Newsletter — activates day one, on Substack.** Different from the original plan, which deferred the newsletter to month 3. Putting Substack as the publication home means the newsletter activates immediately — every Substack post emails subscribers. Send a curated monthly digest as a separate "From the Editor"-style issue starting month two: roundup of recent pieces plus one chart that didn't appear elsewhere.

**Long-form non-format pieces — handful per year.** The pipeline origin story (week 2), the visual library piece (week 3), an annual "year in cities" retrospective, a state-of-housing piece, an annual Overheating Index update. These signal craft and methodology rigor to the hiring-manager audience specifically.

**X content between pieces.** Chart-of-the-week (one repurposed visual from a recent piece). Behind-the-scenes data finds — interesting Census quirks, surprising county-level numbers, a map preview from a piece in flight. Light commentary on news that touches the editorial pillars (housing market data releases, BLS reports, ACS updates). Threads adapting Data Takes for X-native readers. Quote-tweets of journalists or planners with one added data point.

## Consulting — Content Production (Lean)

Anchored by the public methodology, not by content marketing. Effort goes into four artifacts and almost nothing else.

**The standards library on GitHub.** The product. `event_standards.md` exists. Add `property_standards.md` (month 2), `data_quality_rules.md` (month 3), `yaml_contract_standard.md` (month 4), and `good_vs_bad_examples.md` (month 5). Templates and two worked examples by month 6. This repo is the credibility surface — when a B2B PM Googles "tracking plan methodology," it's what they find. It does not require posting on social.

**One long-form piece per month** on the consulting site's blog. Pull from the post bank in `personal_brand_and_portfolio.md`: object_action naming, property families, the four-tier property model, the YAML contract pattern, the cross-functional operating model, the 10 most common tracking plan mistakes. Cross-post selectively to Locally Optimistic. These pieces are sales assets, not feed content.

**The template pack** on Gumroad. Launch at $49 in month three, raise to $99 after 50 sales, settle at $149. Add the Consultant Edition at $499 with white-label rights as the upsell to the secondary ICP (fellow consultants, agencies).

**Three tracking plan teardowns** in months 2–4 for friendly contacts in your network — these become anonymized case studies on the consulting site, not posts.

**Targeted distribution, not broadcast.** When a long-form goes live, share it in The Measure Slack and Locally Optimistic Slack. Reach out directly to 5–10 fractional analytics consultants and small agencies (LinkedIn search "fractional analytics" + "B2B SaaS") with a personal message and a link to the template pack — once, with the Consultant Edition pitch.

**Speaking.** One MeasureCamp per year if scheduling allows. Pitch one product/data-leader podcast per quarter. These compound slowly but the asymmetry is real — one good MeasureCamp talk produces inbound for 12 months.

**No personal LinkedIn feed for consulting.** This is the discipline. Tracking-plan content does not appear on the Patterns in Place-aligned personal feed. If you find yourself wanting to post a consulting insight publicly, write it as a long-form on the consulting site instead.

## Bridge Content

The small but valuable category — pieces that legitimately serve both ventures without being awkward. Aim for one per month. Run them through the personal LinkedIn showcase feed (since they're also relevant to a hiring-manager audience) and on X.

The natural bridges:

- **Data quality and schema discipline.** Census schema drift, ACS confidence intervals, the cost of missing values, the cost of inconsistent geographies — same problem as event-data drift, just at a different scale. A single "what tracking-plan teams can learn from Census data" piece every couple of months works for both audiences.
- **LLM-applied-to-data work.** The chatbot's tool-calling design is genuinely interesting to product analytics teams thinking about AI features. One LinkedIn post per month on this theme bridges both audiences.
- **Methodology and publication discipline as craft topics.** The visual library piece, a piece on why standards docs matter, a piece on the discipline of publishing methodology before findings — these read as craft content for both data publishers and B2B analytics leaders.
- **Geographic / B2B eCom overlap.** A few B2B eCom companies care about geographic segmentation (territory analysis, shipping zones, regional pricing). A tasteful "here's how I'd think about geo data in B2B eCom" post pulls from both.
- **The dual-venture origin story itself.** Once or twice a year — a piece on what it's like to run an independent publication and a small consulting practice in parallel. This is the piece that hiring managers and curious peers will share and remember.

The discipline: bridge pieces are framed for the urban data audience first, with the B2B angle as the secondary read. Don't reverse the framing — that's what dilutes the publication's identity.

## Weekly Rhythm

A realistic 15-hour week, sustained:

- **Monday (1 hr):** weekly plan; check inbound on both ventures; queue X posts for the week
- **Tuesday (2 hrs):** Patterns in Place Data Take draft (the smallest, most reliable piece)
- **Wednesday (1 hr):** consulting work — one slot for inbound calls or one slot for a standards-doc page
- **Thursday (2 hrs):** Patterns in Place — Metro Deep Dive or Opportunity List work, whichever is in flight
- **Friday (1 hr):** content distribution — publish the Data Take to Substack, post the X thread, update the newsletter draft
- **Saturday (4 hrs):** longer block, mostly Patterns in Place — pipeline work, tool deployment, or Metro Deep Dive writing
- **Sunday (4 hrs):** longer block — Patterns in Place tool/visual work, plus any spillover for consulting

When a Foundation Build is in flight, the consulting hours absorb to 12–15/week for four weeks; Patterns in Place shrinks to one Data Take + one X thread per week and that's it. After delivery, swing back to the standard rhythm.

## Decision Triggers and Checkpoints

The structure has explicit checkpoints to avoid drifting into either over-investing in a failing path or under-investing in a working one.

**Month 4 — Substack growth check.** If Substack is netting 50+ new subscribers/month organically, the LinkedIn-light architecture is working — keep going. If under 10/month, the discovery isn't happening on Substack and X alone — reintroduce LinkedIn at higher cadence (4–6 posts/month, treating LinkedIn as a real distribution channel rather than just a showcase). Decide at this checkpoint, not earlier, not later.

**Month 6 — consulting viability check.** If the consulting practice has produced 1+ paid Foundation Build and the template pack is earning $200+/month, the lean structure is working. If it has produced zero paid work, the diagnosis is one of three things: warm-network outreach didn't happen (fix: do the 30-people conversation), the consulting site is invisible (fix: invest a weekend in SEO and site polish), or the practice needs to be even smaller (fix: drop to two services, raise prices, accept that it's a once-or-twice-a-year thing).

**Month 6 — Patterns in Place inbound check.** If Patterns in Place has produced at least one meaningful inbound conversation (a press citation, a hiring manager reaching out, a partnership inquiry, a speaking invitation), the publication is doing its career-progression job. If zero, the diagnosis is usually cadence (ship more Data Takes, faster) or pillar drift (re-read `editorial_strategy.md`).

**Month 9 — Stage 3 fork check.** By month nine you should be able to answer: which Stage 3 path from `growth_roadmap.md` excites you most? (Independent publication with newsletter monetization, tools-first product, civic/academic research outfit, or senior in-house role with the publication as a platform.) If you can't answer, you're not done with Stage 1/2 yet — keep shipping. If you can answer, start optimizing toward it.

**Anytime — the "drop one venture" trigger.** Drop the consulting practice if: a senior in-house role with strict moonlighting limits is the right move; the publication grows faster than expected and the consulting hours would be better spent there; or after 12 months you've concluded B2B eCom isn't the audience you want to serve. Drop Patterns in Place — really drop it, don't half-life it — if: you've gone 90 days without shipping anything; you stopped enjoying it; the consulting grew into a real revenue base that demands the time. The published methodology in either case stays valuable as a passive asset.

## Risks and What to Watch

**Risk 1 — Substack alone doesn't reach the audiences.** X reach for organic data content is meaningfully smaller than it was three years ago. Bluesky helps for civic tech but is small overall. If the publication isn't growing, the failure mode is usually "the channel mix is wrong" rather than "the writing is wrong." The month-4 checkpoint is exactly to catch this.

**Risk 2 — LinkedIn-light gives up the hiring-manager audience.** Most US hiring managers live on LinkedIn. The showcase posts have to actually showcase. If the 1–2 posts a month are weak, the architecture fails for the interview funnel even if the publication grows. Watch what the showcase posts look like; they need to be the strongest visual or finding from each big piece, not afterthoughts.

**Risk 3 — The consulting business goes silent because there's no feed marketing.** Expected. The consulting practice should be running on warm-network outreach, GitHub-repo discoverability, and selective community presence — not on social. If after six months you have zero conversations, escalate consulting distribution (separate company-page LinkedIn presence, MeasureCamp speaking, agency partnership outreach) or accept that the practice needs to be even smaller in scope.

**Risk 4 — A Foundation Build absorbs three weeks and the publication goes dark.** This is the failure mode that kills the publication. Mitigation: the weekly Data Take is non-negotiable. Even in a Foundation Build week, one Data Take ships. Build a backlog of 4+ Data Takes drafted ahead at all times so a busy week doesn't kill cadence.

**Risk 5 — Pseudonymity at the publication brand level breaks the interview funnel.** The X handle is `@patternsinplace`, the Substack is `patternsinplace.substack.com`, and the publication brand is the front. But your name appears on bylines, on the About page, and on your LinkedIn profile. A hiring manager who sees the publication needs to find you in two clicks. Don't let the pseudonymity slide into actual anonymity.

**Risk 6 — Bridge content dilutes both audiences.** Bridge pieces should be rare (one a month at most) and framed for the urban data audience first. If you find yourself writing bridge content more often than that, you're probably trying to make the consulting business work via the publication's feed — which is exactly what this architecture is designed to avoid.

**Risk 7 — Burnout from running two ventures at once.** The 70/30 split is sustainable; trying to run both at full strength is not. If you find yourself working 25+ hours a week consistently and still feeling behind, drop the consulting cadence further (2 Foundation Builds a year instead of 4; one long-form every two months instead of one a month) or pause it entirely for a quarter.

## The Single Highest-Leverage Move This Week

If only one thing happens this week, make it this: **claim the Patterns in Place Substack publication, GitHub org, X handle, and Bluesky handle, and publish the publication README on the GitHub org and as the Substack About page.** This is two hours of work that turns the dual-venture plan from a document into a publication that exists in the world. Everything else in this doc compounds from that moment.

The second-highest-leverage move: **set the weekly Data Take rhythm in motion.** Pick the most surprising question from the chatbot Q&A training set, write the 700-word Data Take, ship it on Substack, and post the X thread within four hours. The first Data Take is the smallest possible proof that the publication is real and shipping. Once you've done it once, the cadence is just doing it again next week.

The rest of the architecture — tools, Metro Deep Dives, the consulting standards docs, the showcase LinkedIn posts, the bridge content — all compounds from those two moves.
