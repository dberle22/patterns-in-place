## X Thread

**Post 1**  
Housing vacancy is low in a lot of big US metros. Extremely high vacancy is much more concentrated.

Among major metros in the 50 states and DC, the 20 tightest housing markets in 2024 ran from `3.0%` to `5.1%`, all below the US vacancy rate of `10.1%`.

**Post 2**  
That tight list is broader and more mixed than the usual stereotype suggests.

`Lancaster, PA`, `Provo-Orem-Lehi, UT`, `Modesto, CA`, and `Greeley, CO` are all near the very bottom, and the full top 20 spans the Northeast, West, Midwest, and Mountain West.

**Post 3**  
The loose side tells a different story.

The 20 loosest major metros ranged from `13.9%` to `33.4%`, led by `Atlantic City-Hammonton, NJ`, `Naples-Marco Island, FL`, and `Seaford, DE`.

**Post 4**  
Those high-vacancy metros are much more concentrated in seasonal, tourism-heavy, and retirement-oriented places, especially in Florida.

So the big picture is not just “some blue metros are tight.” It is that low vacancy is common across large metros, while very high vacancy is concentrated in a smaller and more distinctive set of markets.

## Substack Caption

The 2024 metro vacancy rankings show an asymmetric housing market. Among major metros in the 50 states and DC, the tightest 20 markets were all tightly clustered between `3.0%` and `5.1%`, well below the US vacancy rate of `10.1%`. That suggests low housing slack is not limited to one narrow slice of the country.

The loosest major metros looked very different. Their vacancy rates ranged from `13.9%` to `33.4%`, with the highest values concentrated in a smaller set of seasonal, tourism-oriented, and retirement-heavy markets. Taken together, the charts suggest that tight housing conditions are broadly distributed across large metros, while unusually loose conditions are much more geographically concentrated.
