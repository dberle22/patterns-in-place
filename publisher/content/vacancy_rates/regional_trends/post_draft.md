## X Thread

**Post 1**  
Every Census region has tightened since 2019, but the regional order barely changed.

From 2019 to 2024, vacancy fell in all four regions: `South` from `14.0%` to `11.7%`, `Northeast` from `11.8%` to `9.6%`, `Midwest` from `11.4%` to `9.4%`, and `West` from `10.0%` to `8.5%`.

**Post 2**  
The West was already the tightest region before the recent compression, and it stayed there the whole time.

By 2024 it was still well below the US vacancy rate, while the South remained clearly above it.

**Post 3**  
The South actually posted the biggest absolute drop, but it still ended up as the loosest region.

That is a useful reminder that the places tightening fastest are not always the places that are tightest today.

**Post 4**  
The big picture is shared tightening, not full convergence.

The gap between the tightest and loosest regions narrowed, but it was still `3.1` percentage points in 2024. So the housing crunch is national, even if it still looks meaningfully different across regions.

## Substack Caption

The regional vacancy chart shows that housing slack has been shrinking across the entire country, not just in one or two hot markets. Every Census region saw vacancy fall from 2019 to 2024, with the biggest declines in the `South` and `Northeast`.

But the chart also shows how persistent the regional hierarchy is. The `West` remained the tightest region throughout the period, while the `South` remained the loosest even after a substantial decline. That means the national housing squeeze is broad, but longstanding regional differences in available housing slack have not disappeared.
