## X Thread

**Post 1**  
The US housing market has gotten materially tighter.

National vacancy held around 12.1%-12.5% for most of the 2010s, then fell every year after 2019, dropping from 12.1% to 10.1% by 2024.

**Post 2**  
This matters because vacancy is one of the clearest signals of housing slack.

When vacancy falls, it usually means fewer homes are available relative to demand, which puts more pressure on prices and rents.

**Post 3**  
The shift is also broad, not just a quirk in one part of the country.

A housing-unit-weighted average across US metros also moved down, from 11.3% in 2019 to 9.4% in 2024.

**Post 4**  
The big picture: the 2010s looked stable. The 2020s look tighter every single year.

That helps explain why housing feels so constrained even before you zoom in to individual states or metros.

## Substack Caption

For most of the 2010s, the US vacancy rate barely moved. It stayed in a narrow 12.1%-12.5% range from 2012 through 2019, suggesting a national housing market that was relatively stable on this measure.

That changed after 2019. National vacancy fell every year from 12.1% in 2019 to 10.1% in 2024, a 2.0 percentage point decline in just five years. A housing-unit-weighted metro average fell too, reinforcing the idea that this is a broad tightening in housing slack rather than a story isolated to a few markets.
