# Summary — State Vacancy Map

Housing vacancy in 2024 was lowest in a cluster of coastal and land-constrained
states, led by `Connecticut` at `7.0%`, followed by `Washington` at `7.3%` and
`New Jersey`, `California`, and `Oregon` at `7.5%`. That pattern suggests the
tightest state housing markets are concentrated in places where new supply is
harder to add quickly.

The loosest states looked very different. `Maine` (`20.6%`) and `Vermont`
(`19.4%`) stood apart from the rest of the map, followed by `West Virginia`
(`15.4%`), `Florida` (`14.7%`), and `Mississippi` (`14.6%`). The contrast
reinforces the broader thesis of the series: housing slack is disappearing
nationally, but the level of remaining slack still varies sharply across states.
