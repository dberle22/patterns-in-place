## X Thread

**Post 1**  
The states with the tightest housing markets in 2024 were mostly coastal and land-constrained.

`Connecticut` had the lowest vacancy rate at `7.0%`, followed by `Washington` at `7.3%` and `New Jersey`, `California`, and `Oregon` at `7.5%`.

**Post 2**  
That is a very different pattern from the loosest states.

`Maine` led the country at `20.6%`, followed by `Vermont` at `19.4%`, then `West Virginia` (`15.4%`), `Florida` (`14.7%`), and `Mississippi` (`14.6%`).

**Post 3**  
So the state map is not just showing “tight everywhere.”

It shows a national market with much less housing slack overall, but with remaining slack still concentrated in a smaller and very different group of states.

**Post 4**  
That split helps explain why the housing story feels so uneven across the country.

In some states, vacancy is already extremely low. In others, there is still more slack, but often in markets shaped by second homes, seasonal demand, or weaker long-run housing pressure.

## Substack Caption

The 2024 state vacancy map shows a clear geographic split. The lowest vacancy
rates were concentrated in coastal and land-constrained states, led by
`Connecticut` at `7.0%`, `Washington` at `7.3%`, and a three-way `7.5%` cluster
of `New Jersey`, `California`, and `Oregon`. In these places, housing slack is
already quite limited.

The highest vacancy rates were concentrated in a very different set of states.
`Maine` (`20.6%`) and `Vermont` (`19.4%`) were far above the rest of the map,
followed by `West Virginia` (`15.4%`), `Florida` (`14.7%`), and `Mississippi`
(`14.6%`). The result is a national picture where housing has tightened broadly,
but the amount of slack that remains still varies sharply from one state to the
next.
