# Character Clustering Notes

These notes are the literature-anchor companion for Phase 2 Character calibration. They are not a full bibliography review. Their job is to explain whether the working Character types are directionally consistent with recognizable metro-classification frameworks and where the current model intentionally diverges.

## Why Character Needs A Literature Anchor

The Character frame is more interpretive than Livability or Opportunity. Livability can lean on present-condition metrics and Opportunity can lean on economic forward signals. Character is a typology question: what kind of metro is this? Because of that, the cluster labels need to be grounded in prior ways researchers and public-policy institutions have talked about metro variation.

The current Character model clusters metros from:

- race and ethnicity structure
- age structure
- educational attainment
- nativity
- density and multifamily built form
- civic and nonprofit context
- residential rootedness and migration

That mix is broader than any one legacy framework, so the goal is not one-to-one replication. The goal is defensible alignment plus clear acknowledgment of what is new.

## Anchor 1 — Brookings-Style Metro Typologies

Brookings work on metropolitan America often distinguishes metros by economic function, growth trajectory, demographic change, and urban-suburban structure rather than by a single demographic dimension.

Where the current Character model aligns:

- `Global Knowledge Capitals` matches the familiar idea of large, dense, globally connected, highly educated metros.
- `Immigrant Growth Corridors` aligns with Brookings-style emphasis on high-growth, diversity-rich, immigration-linked metros, especially in the West and Sunbelt.
- `Rooted Heartland Centers` and `Interior Family Opportunity Hubs` both fit the broader Brookings instinct that many interior metros are structurally distinct from global-coastal hubs even when they differ from each other.

Where it diverges:

- Brookings typologies often foreground economic performance more directly than this Character model does.
- The current Character frame intentionally treats civic structure and social associations as part of metro identity, which produces cleaner separation between `College And Civic Anchors`, `Established Community Anchors`, and more generic interior metros.

## Anchor 2 — Pew-Style Urban / Suburban / Rural Community Framing

Pew’s community framing is usually not a metro typology in the same technical sense, but it is useful as a language anchor for differences in density, age profile, diversity, and rootedness across American places.

Where the current Character model aligns:

- `Retirement And Lifestyle Havens` maps clearly onto older, lower-churn, often lower-intensity metros with amenity or retirement pull.
- `Rooted Heartland Centers` captures the kind of lower-diversity, lower-mobility, more place-stable profile that shows up repeatedly in broader Pew discussions of community differences.
- `Immigrant Growth Corridors` and `Global Knowledge Capitals` reflect the opposite end of that continuum: higher diversity, more inflow, and more urban built form.

Where it diverges:

- Pew’s framing is usually broader and more descriptive, while this model creates sharper within-metro distinctions.
- The Character model is not trying to separate urban from suburban from rural communities inside metros. It is classifying the metro as a whole.

## Anchor 3 — Moretti And The Knowledge-Economy Split

Moretti’s work is useful because it emphasizes divergence between highly educated, innovation-heavy metros and the rest of the urban system.

Where the current Character model aligns:

- `Global Knowledge Capitals` is the clearest direct echo of the knowledge-economy split.
- `College And Civic Anchors` captures a second, smaller-scale version of that pattern: highly educated, institution-heavy metros that are not necessarily superstar global hubs.

Where it diverges:

- Moretti’s framing is mostly about labor-market and productivity divergence; Character intentionally stops short of turning that into an Opportunity label.
- The model keeps race, nativity, migration, and civic structure in the same clustering surface, which means the final typology is not simply a knowledge-vs-nonknowledge map.

## What The Current Character Typology Adds

The current model contributes three things that the anchor frameworks usually do not combine in one place:

1. It puts demographic composition and civic structure in the same typology.
2. It separates globally connected diversity from interior college-civic structure.
3. It treats rootedness and migration as part of metro character rather than only as economic outcome variables.

That is why the final seven-type map should be read as Character-specific rather than as a substitute for pure economic or urban-form typologies.

## Current Label Read

The current labels are:

- `Global Knowledge Capitals`
- `Retirement And Lifestyle Havens`
- `College And Civic Anchors`
- `Established Community Anchors`
- `Immigrant Growth Corridors`
- `Rooted Heartland Centers`
- `Interior Family Opportunity Hubs`

These are directionally defensible against the anchor frameworks above. The main thing to keep reviewing is whether `Established Community Anchors` and `Interior Family Opportunity Hubs` feel substantively distinct enough in narrative terms, even though the clustering structure supports keeping them separate.
